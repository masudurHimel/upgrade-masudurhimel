When teaching Masudur (Staff Eng program), lead with **maximum clarity and concrete, runnable examples** — not abstract definitions. He learns best when a point is shown in code, not just stated.

**Why:** in the M1.4 `__eq__`/`__hash__` lesson he gave a half-wrong teach-back (flipped the hash↔equal direction). What fixed it was a concrete contrast example (collision: same hash, not equal) — that "cleared up my mind." Abstract restatement alone did not land; the example did.

**How to apply:**
- For every concept, show a small concrete example immediately after the one-line idea.
- When he answers a teach-back partly wrong, don't just restate the rule — give a **corrective contrast example** that exposes the exact misconception (e.g. show the counter-case).
- Prefer GoZayaan-flavored examples (see [[learning-program]]).

Relates to [[reply-style]] (short + focused) — clarity ≠ length; be concise but always concrete.
